### § FAQ

**Q1. Is Battle Point Park going away?**
The active recreational uses of the parcel are being relocated, not removed. The Replacement Soccer Field Program will deliver two regulation-grade fields in partnership with Bainbridge Parks, with siting confirmed during the public comment process.

**Q2. How much water will the campus use?**
The Battle Point Campus uses a closed-loop seawater cooling system that returns water to Eagle Harbor within one degree Celsius of intake. Freshwater draw on the island aquifer is approximately 92% lower than a comparable inland campus.

**Q3. Will this increase ferry traffic?**
Net-neutral after Phase 1, following the launch of our employee shuttle program. Construction-period crossings will be staged off-peak in coordination with Washington State Ferries.

**Q4. What does this do to property values?**
Independent analyses of the Microsoft Quincy and adjacent regional campuses indicate a modest positive long-term impact within the surrounding residential market. We are commissioning a localized study during the comment period.

**Q5. Who is Olympic Compute Group?**
Olympic Compute Group is a Pacific Northwest infrastructure company headquartered in Seattle. The Battle Point Campus is our first announced facility.

**Q6. Will I be able to see the building from the water?**
No. The master plan preserves the existing tree line along the Salish Sea side. Mechanical penthouse equipment is screened to remain below the surrounding canopy height.

**Q7. What about the deer?**
The perimeter is engineered with wildlife-permeable corridors at the north and east edges, designed in consultation with state wildlife biologists. Existing deer migration routes through the parcel are preserved.

**Q8. How do I submit a comment?**
Email the Community Liaison at `community.liaison.ocg@gmail.com` at any time, or attend an open-house session during the June 15 – August 30 comment window.

## `/sustainability` page

**h1:** Sustainability at the Battle Point Campus

**intro paragraph:** The most sustainable kilowatt-hour is the one designed correctly the first time. Every system at the Battle Point Campus — water, carbon, materials, light, sound — is sized, sourced, and commissioned to a single standard: that the campus leaves the island measurably better than it found it. The pages that follow describe how.

### Water

The Battle Point Campus is cooled by a fully closed-loop seawater exchange drawn from Eagle Harbor. Cold-side intake passes through a redundant filtration array, exchanges heat through a sealed titanium plate system, and returns to the Sound within one degree Celsius of intake temperature. No evaporative loss. No discharge of process chemicals. The intake and outfall structures are sited and engineered in active coordination with state and federal sediment-management protocols, and all construction-phase shoreline work is bonded to a long-tail monitoring program extending fifteen years beyond commissioning.

The result, measured against a comparably sized inland AI campus, is a roughly 92% reduction in freshwater draw and the elimination of cooling-tower plume. The island's aquifer is untouched. The Sound itself, we believe, is the largest piece of cooling infrastructure ever engineered into a Pacific Northwest data center — and one of the most carefully governed.

### Carbon

Phase 1 operations will run on 100% carbon-free electricity sourced through the Bonneville Power Administration's predominantly hydroelectric mix, one of the cleanest grid resources in North America. We do not rely on unbundled renewable energy credits to make that claim; the campus draws from the regional grid that already runs clean.

Embodied carbon — the carbon released to manufacture and build the campus itself — is targeted 40% below the 2024 industry baseline. Concrete mixes are specified with high supplementary cementitious materials content. Structural steel is sourced from electric-arc furnaces running on renewable power. The result is a campus comparable in computational scale to Microsoft's Quincy facility, with a substantially lower lifetime carbon footprint.

### Place

A data center is a building, but it is also a neighbor. The Battle Point Campus is designed as one. The forest canopy on the surrounding parcel is preserved and placed under perpetual stewardship through the Battle Point Forest Steward Endowment. The shoreline buffer is restored with native understory. Exterior lighting is dark-sky compliant and downcast. Mechanical equipment is acoustically shrouded to a property-line sound contour quieter than the prevailing wind through the Doug firs.

We measure place the way the island does — in birds counted, in trails maintained, in mornings spent at the farmers market — and we have committed to publishing those measurements annually, alongside the kilowatt-hours.

## `/community-partnership` page

**h1:** Community Partnership

**intro paragraph:** Olympic Compute Group commits $2.4M annually to Bainbridge Island in direct community investment for the operating life of the Battle Point Campus. The programs below are designed in consultation with island stakeholders and structured to be administered by, and accountable to, the community itself. We acknowledge the ancestral and treaty-protected lands of the Suquamish people, and we are in early consultation with the Tribe regarding cultural and shoreline considerations.

### Battle Point Forest Steward Endowment

A perpetual endowment funding canopy restoration, native understory replanting, and trail stewardship across the surrounding parcel. The endowment is administered by a community board that includes Bainbridge Parks staff, a Suquamish Tribe–nominated seat, a community-nominated seat selected through a public application process, and one Olympic Compute Group representative. Annual stewardship reports are published publicly. The endowment principal is structured so that distributions continue at full real value for the operating life of the campus and beyond.

### Sound-to-Server STEM Scholarship

Four-year full-tuition scholarships for graduating Bainbridge High School seniors pursuing computer science, electrical engineering, or marine systems studies at any accredited Washington institution. Recipients are selected by an independent panel that includes Bainbridge educators and local STEM professionals. The program is sized to extend through the operating life of the campus and is indexed to in-state tuition rates so that the award keeps pace with the cost of education.

### Replacement Soccer Field Program

Two regulation-grade replacement fields, delivered in partnership with Bainbridge Parks at no cost to the City. Siting will be confirmed in collaboration with Parks during the public comment process. The replacement program is funded through campus construction and is structured to deliver fields ready for play prior to the displacement of existing recreational use.

### Civic and cultural partnerships

A dedicated annual partnership budget supports island institutions including the Bainbridge Island Museum of Art, IslandWood, the Bainbridge Public Library, and the year-round farmers market, with allocations refreshed annually in consultation with each organization. The intent is straightforward: the institutions that already define this community do the defining. We provide steady, predictable support.
