# Visual Prompts — Olympic Compute Group / Battle Point Campus

> Generated by the Visual Team for the Olympic Compute Group satire microsite. The site lives at `https://olympic-compute.vercel.app`. These prompts are written to feed into Midjourney v6/v7, Flux 1.1 Pro, Sora image, gpt-image-1, Ideogram 3, or Nano Banana — pick whichever the human prefers. Each block has a primary prompt + two alternates so the human can iterate if the first render doesn't land.
>
> **Drop output files into the `public/` paths listed below — filenames are stable; code references them by name.**
>
> Personas (in order): A) Architectural visualization artist, B) Site plan designer, C) Interior render artist, D) Brand / logomark designer.

## Persona A — Architectural Visualization Artist

> *"My job is to make the unbuilt look inevitable. Press renders for hyperscale campuses (Microsoft Quincy, Meta Eagle Mountain, Google Council Bluffs, AWS Boardman) all share a vocabulary: drone altitude, slight tele-compression, overcast PNW or clean golden-hour light, a white-roof building so geometrically pure it reads as 'engineered, not built,' and a perimeter that whispers 'serious infrastructure.' If I do this correctly, a Bainbridge Islander seeing this image for the first time will feel the bottom drop out of their stomach before they finish reading the headline. The fear we're triggering: 'the trees behind the soccer fields are still there, which means this is real.' The recognizable shoreline of Maisy Beach and the Venice neighborhood north of the park are the load-bearing satire — they prove the geography is correct, which means the building must be too."*

### Asset A1 — Hero aerial composite

- **File:** `public/hero-aerial.jpg`
- **Dimensions:** 1920 × 1080 (16:9)
- **Format:** JPG, sRGB, quality ≥ 90, target file size < 600 KB after `next/image` optimization

**Primary prompt (Midjourney v6 / Flux 1.1 Pro):**

```
Photorealistic aerial drone photograph, 600ft altitude looking northwest over Bainbridge Island, Washington, late afternoon overcast Pacific Northwest light, soft diffused sky. Center frame: a single-story hyperscale data center campus, approximately 412,000 square feet, rectangular white TPO roof with neat rows of rooftop mechanical units and a long central penthouse, slate-grey steel cladding at ground level, four loading bays on the east elevation. Built on a clearcut rectangular pad where soccer fields and a baseball diamond used to be, surrounded on three sides by dense mature Douglas fir and western red cedar forest. A dual electrical substation with two transformer banks hugs the south tree line, ringed by chain-link fence with razor wire. A wide gravel-and-asphalt access road curves in from the east. To the west, a narrow sand-and-driftwood shoreline along Puget Sound (Maisy Beach) with calm grey-blue water; to the north, a residential neighborhood of low-pitched roofs in a rectilinear street grid (the Venice neighborhood). Crisp, documentary, architectural press-release style — think Microsoft datacenter, Meta press kit, Google data center gallery. No vehicles, no people. Cinematic but restrained, telephoto compression, neutral color grade with slightly desaturated greens.
--ar 16:9 --style raw --v 6
```

**Negatives / avoid:** lens flare, HDR halos, fantasy lighting, sci-fi towers, multi-story buildings, glass facades, blue glow from inside the building, smoke, helicopters, watermark, text overlay, illustrative or cartoon rendering, exaggerated tilt-shift miniature effect.

**Alternate phrasing 1 (Flux, more documentary):**

```
Aerial photo, Bainbridge Island Washington, late October overcast. Top-down 30-degree oblique, focal length ~85mm equivalent. A newly-built single-story hyperscale AI compute facility, white reflective roof, ~412,000 sqft footprint, occupying the former Battle Point Park recreational fields. Forest preserved on north, east, south perimeters; Puget Sound shoreline visible on the west edge of frame. Twin substation with high-voltage gantries on the south side. Tan gravel staging yard, neat striped parking lot for ~400 vehicles east of the building. Photographic realism, color-graded for a corporate sustainability report. No text, no logos visible.
```

**Alternate phrasing 2 (gpt-image-1, prose-style):**

```
Generate a photorealistic aerial image, as if taken from a survey drone at 600 feet, of a newly constructed hyperscale data center on Bainbridge Island, Washington. The building is a single rectangular block with a white roof and grey walls, set on a clearcut pad where soccer fields used to be. Mature evergreen forest wraps the building on three sides; the Puget Sound shoreline is visible at the left edge of the frame, and a grid of residential streets at the top edge. A two-bank electrical substation sits at the south end of the property. The mood is calm, late-afternoon, overcast Pacific Northwest, similar to a press release photo for a Microsoft or Meta data center campus. No people or vehicles. Realistic, documentary, not illustrated.
```

**Rationale:** This is the single most load-bearing image on the site. The satire only works if a viewer's first reaction is *"wait — that's the soccer field"*. Keeping Maisy Beach, the Venice grid, and the surrounding tree line correct is non-negotiable. The hyperscaler press-render visual grammar (white roof, substation, perimeter fence) does most of the work — the brain instantly classifies it as "real corporate announcement" before the conscious mind catches up.

## Persona B — Site Plan Designer

> *"Architectural master plans have a specific visual language: muted earth-tone fills, hairline strokes, a tidy title block, a north arrow, a graphic scale bar. The trick is making the phases legible at thumbnail size and at full-screen zoom — at thumbnail you should read 'phased campus,' at full zoom you should read 'oh god they planned for expansion.' I'm using stock master-plan greens and greys deliberately because real plans from real firms (HOK, Gensler, NBBJ) all share this palette. The satire grenade buried in this asset is the parking grid — 400 stalls is what you build when you mean it."*

## Persona C — Interior Render Artist

> *"The instinct here is to make the server hall feel cold and dystopian — that's the wrong instinct. The satire works BETTER when the interior looks aspirational, like a Meta press shot. The viewer should feel a small, traitorous twinge of 'oh, that's actually beautiful' before remembering this is sitting on top of where their kid played t-ball. The blue downlight + vanishing-point composition is the universal hyperscaler iconography — every press kit on earth has this shot."*

### Asset C1 — Server hall interior

- **File:** `public/interior-render.jpg`
- **Dimensions:** 1920 × 1080
- **Format:** JPG, sRGB, quality ≥ 88

**Primary prompt:**

```
Photorealistic interior of a hyperscale AI compute hall, taken with a 24mm wide-angle lens at chest height, centered down a long server aisle for strong one-point perspective. Two parallel rows of black server racks, ~7 feet tall, extending toward a distant vanishing point. Cold-aisle containment overhead — glass or polycarbonate panels capping the aisle. Polished concrete floor with subtle specular reflections. Downlighting is cool blue-white (~5000K, slightly cyan-tinted), with thin warm amber accent strips at the floor line of each rack for contrast. A faint atmospheric haze (clean, not smoky) catches the light. Server cabinet doors closed, neat blue LED indicator strips visible. A single human figure in a hi-vis vest, hard hat, and dark trousers stands ~80 feet down the aisle for scale, facing slightly away from camera. No cables visible, no clutter, no signage with text. Documentary-architectural photography style, slight anamorphic flare, depth of field shallow on the figure. Press-release aesthetic, similar to published photos of Meta Eagle Mountain or AWS US-East data halls.
--ar 16:9 --style raw --v 6
```

**Negatives / avoid:** dystopian red lighting, smoke, sparks, broken equipment, exposed wires, server rack doors hanging open, multiple people, text/logos on racks, fisheye distortion, fluorescent green glow, cyberpunk aesthetic, neon.

**Alternate phrasing 1 (more cinematic):**

```
Photograph: vast modern data center hall, two rows of identical black server racks vanishing into distance, cold blue overhead lighting, polished concrete floor, one engineer in PPE walking far down the aisle, calm and orderly, beautiful, press-release style, wide-angle one-point perspective, slight haze, no text or branding visible.
```

**Alternate phrasing 2 (architectural, less moody):**

```
Interior of a state-of-the-art AI data center, single-point perspective looking down a clean aisle of black server cabinets, cold-aisle containment ceiling panels, neutral cool lighting, single technician in safety vest for scale at the end of the aisle. Photorealistic, documentary, restrained, similar to a corporate sustainability report cover.
```

**Rationale:** This section of the homepage is where the corporate veneer is thickest. The interior shot lets a skeptical visitor reassure themselves: *"this is just what data centers look like, this must be real."* Aspirational beats dystopian.

## Persona D — Brand / Logomark Designer

> *"The current mark in `src/components/logo.tsx` is a 4-peak zigzag rendered as a stroked open path — fine as a placeholder, but it's neither closed nor balanced. A real hyperscaler mark would be a solid, closed silhouette that reads at 16px. Direction: keep the 4-peak Olympic-range silhouette (it's the load-bearing geographic pun — Olympic Mountains = Olympic Compute), but tighten the proportions, close the path, and tuck a CPU-die motif into one of the peaks. Evergreen on light, bone on dark. The wordmark stays simple Inter SemiBold — no custom letterforms, because hyperscalers don't do bespoke type."*

### Asset D1 — Logomark (refined SVG)

- **File:** `public/ocg-logo.svg`
- **Dimensions:** 200 × 48 viewBox (mark + wordmark lockup); the mark alone is 32 × 32
- **Format:** SVG, hand-authored (no image-gen needed — see SVG below in Output 2)

**Direction for image-gen (only if a designer wants to iterate):**

```
A minimalist corporate logomark: a solid silhouette of four mountain peaks forming a continuous range, in the style of Olympic Mountains seen from Puget Sound. Embedded in the largest central peak, a small square CPU die motif with eight pin-stubs on each side (subtle, almost invisible at small sizes). Single color: deep evergreen (#2a4a3a). Geometric, precise, no shadows, no gradients. Should read clearly at 16x16 favicon size. Vector style, flat. To the right of the mark, the wordmark "Olympic Compute Group" in Inter SemiBold, kerned tight, same evergreen color.
```

### Asset D2 — Favicon

- **Files:** `public/favicon.ico` (multi-size: 32×32, 16×16) and `public/icon.svg` (1024×1024 source)
- **Format:** ICO for the legacy favicon; modern Next.js prefers `icon.svg` or `icon.png` at the App Router level
- **Approach:** Export the mark-only (no wordmark) at 1024×1024 as SVG, then use `realfavicongenerator.net` or `npx --yes png-to-ico mark-32.png mark-16.png > favicon.ico` to produce the multi-size .ico.

**Rationale:** The brand mark must survive at 16×16 in a browser tab. A solid silhouette does that; an open stroke does not. The Olympic-range pun is the only piece of brand wit on the entire site — everything else is dead-serious corporate.
