# Olympic Compute Group — 3-Persona Review Panel
Reviewed: 2026-06-02
Production URL: https://olympic-compute.vercel.app

## Reviewer R1 — "Local who knows the prank"
**Score: 9/10 (funniness)**
**Threshold: ≥ 8 to ship.**

Oh man. I read this on the ferry coming back from Colman and I had to put the phone down. They went there. Battle Point. The fields my kid has been kicking a ball on since he was four. They didn't just pick a sacred cow — they picked the soccer fields, and then they offered to *replace* them. "Two regulation-grade replacement fields, delivered in partnership with Bainbridge Parks at no cost to the City." That sentence killed me. The casual "at no cost to the City" — like the city was worried about the invoice and not the part where you bulldoze the park.

The bingo card hits are perfect. Eagle Harbor — *Eagle Harbor*, a literal Superfund site with creosote in the sediment — is now "the largest piece of cooling infrastructure ever engineered into a Pacific Northwest data center." That's a hall-of-fame line. "We measure place the way the island does — in birds counted, in trails maintained, in mornings spent at the farmers market" is the kind of sentence I would expect to see laminated at the Town & Country checkout. And FAQ #7, "What about the deer?" — "wildlife-permeable corridors at the north and east edges, designed in consultation with state wildlife biologists" — perfectly captures every Bainbridge planning meeting I've ever sat through.

The Bloedel/IslandWood namecheck in Why Bainbridge ("a civic culture that thinks in generations") is so on-the-nose it almost telegraphs, but for an outsider reader it lands as authentic. The Suquamish land acknowledgment is doing real work — it's the move a real corporate comms team would absolutely make, and it's what tips this from parody into camouflage. The "Sound-to-Server STEM Scholarship" name is chef's kiss.

Only soft spot: the "deer" FAQ is the one moment the wink almost lands too hard. Anyone who knows Bainbridge knows the deer are the running joke. But it's funny enough I'd keep it.

SHIP.

## Reviewer R2 — "Local who doesn't know"
**Score: 8/10 (plausibility)**
**Threshold: ≥ 7 to ship.**

I'm sitting at my kitchen table and I don't know what to do with this. A friend forwarded the link this morning with "did you see this???" and I've now read all four pages twice. My stomach is in a knot. They're putting a 412,000 square foot data center on Battle Point. *Battle Point.* Where my daughter's team plays. Where I walk the dog every Sunday morning.

What scares me is how reasonable it sounds. It isn't a sleazy press release. It reads like a Microsoft sustainability page. They acknowledge the Suquamish Tribe. They name Bloedel and IslandWood and the farmers market. They have an *endowment*. They're going to *replace* the soccer fields — "ready for play prior to the displacement of existing recreational use" — which is the kind of sentence that sounds caring but also means *yes, the fields are going away*. The public comment window is real-dated, June 15 to August 30, 2026, and there's a "Community Liaison" email. The press release has a Q3 2027 Phase 1 date. The CEO quote about "a civic culture that thinks in generations" sounds like something I'd read in The Bainbridge Review.

I'm going to forward this to Joan on the City Council. I'm going to post in the Bainbridge Islanders Facebook group. I'm going to email that Community Liaison address and ask who actually owns the parcel — because nothing on the site tells me when they bought the land or whether the City was even consulted before this went public. That's the one thing that's nagging at me. But the rest of it? The Bonneville hydro claim, the Eagle Harbor closed-loop cooling, the 800 union construction jobs, the $2.4M/year community investment — it all reads like a real corporate playbook.

8/10 plausibility. SHIP.

## Reviewer R3 — "Senior DC marketing exec"
**Score: 8/10 (corporate authenticity)**
**Threshold: ≥ 7 to ship.**

This is a remarkably well-executed clone of the modern hyperscaler community-microsite genre. The visual restraint — stone palette, generous whitespace, sans-serif, dark green hero, no stock photos of fiber bundles — matches the Microsoft datacenters.microsoft.com and atmeta.com aesthetic almost exactly. The information architecture (Hero → Why [location] → Site Overview → Sustainability → Community → Jobs → Timeline → FAQ → Press) is the boilerplate I have personally shipped at two of the three big clouds. The bingo terms are all present and used in the right register: "Living Building Challenge–aligned," "closed-loop seawater exchange," "embodied carbon target 40% below the 2024 industry baseline," "Bonneville Power Administration," "Tier-IV-adjacent" is mercifully absent, "comparable in scale to Microsoft's Quincy" is the canonical peer-comparison move. The "we do not rely on unbundled renewable energy credits" line is the kind of pre-bunking sustainability disclosure a real comms team would put in after their first hostile press cycle.

A few tells. (1) The Community Liaison email is `community.liaison.ocg@gmail.com`. Gmail. No real Fortune-500 infra company sends external comms from a Gmail address — this would be `community@olympiccompute.com` or routed through a PR agency. (2) The press release dateline says "June 2, 2026" — the same day someone reads it — which is fine, but there's no contact line for media, no Reg-FD-style boilerplate, no embargo line, no spokesperson named. The blockquote is attributed to "— Olympic Compute Group spokesperson," which no real Tier-1 announcement would do; you'd name the CEO or SVP Infrastructure. (3) No leadership page, no investors page, no "Our story" — a real hyperscaler microsite of this polish would have an executive bio wall by week two. (4) "Engineered from the Salish Sea" in the footer is *almost* too good — a real comms team would have argued that one down to a more sterile tagline.

Compared to atmeta.com/data-centers/ (which is the closest aesthetic peer): Meta runs harder on numbers in the hero and has a visible "community grants" tracker. Microsoft's datacenter community pages always lead with a named local GM. Google's google.com/about/datacenters leans heavily on people-and-places photography. This site reads like a competently-shipped V1 microsite from a regional player ahead of a public comment period — which is, narratively, exactly what it is supposed to be. The Gmail address is the one tell that would flag this to an industry reader within ten seconds. Everything else passes.

8/10 corporate authenticity. SHIP, with a recommendation to swap the Gmail.

## Panel verdict
All three reviewers clear threshold: funniness 9, plausibility 8, authenticity 8. **SHIP.** The site is doing what it needs to do: it reads as a real corporate announcement to a cold local reader (R2 wants to email the city council, which is the desired conversion), it lands the in-jokes for the knowing reader (R1's quoted lines all hit), and it survives a peer-of-the-publisher read with one fixable tell. One recommended fix before wider distribution: replace the `community.liaison.ocg@gmail.com` address with a domain-matched email (e.g. `community@olympiccompute.com`, even if it just forwards). The Gmail is the single highest-leverage authenticity leak — R3 caught it in ten seconds, and a journalist or city staffer would catch it faster. Optional polish: name a spokesperson in the press release blockquote ("— Sarah Chen, VP Community & Place, Olympic Compute Group" or similar) to harden the press page against a quick "who said this?" check. Neither is a blocker. Ship now, harden in a fast-follow.
